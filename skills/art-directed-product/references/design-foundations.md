# Design Foundations

## Subject outward

Build from the product's world: its users, materials, instruments, data, rituals, language, and constraints. The art-direction thesis should explain why the interface could belong to this product and not a neighboring category.

Use real or representative content at realistic density. Let structure follow meaning: sequence, comparison, hierarchy, and status should be visible because the content requires them.

## One signature

Choose one memorable device that carries the thesis: a distinctive composition, type treatment, interaction, visualization, material, or transition. Keep surrounding decisions disciplined so the signature remains legible. A second bold device must support the first rather than compete with it.

## Page model

Match structure to the surface:

- **Campaign:** persuasion may use Attention, Interest, Desire, Action when the content truly follows that progression.
- **Editorial:** reading sequence, provenance, and media rhythm lead.
- **Commerce:** evaluation, trust, comparison, and purchase confidence lead.
- **Application:** task completion, state visibility, density, and recovery lead.
- **Utility:** speed, direct manipulation, and predictable controls lead.

Navigation, heroes, bento grids, pricing blocks, and footers appear only when the product model calls for them.

## Typography

Assign deliberate roles:

- **Display:** character and thesis, used where its personality survives responsive wrapping.
- **Body:** sustained clarity at real reading sizes.
- **Utility:** labels, data, captions, or code where a distinct role is useful.

Select faces from the subject and available licensing rather than a fixed allowlist. Define a responsive scale, line length, weight, width, tracking, and fallback behavior. A campaign hero should usually retain a broad measure and intentional two-to-three-line composition; an application heading should optimize scanning instead.

## Composition and spacing

Treat space as pacing. Campaign chapters may breathe cinematically; operational surfaces may require compact, stable density. Use grids that remain complete at each breakpoint. If content varies, design the grid for the actual variation rather than forcing decorative symmetry.

Responsive layouts may change hierarchy and interaction, not merely shrink. Verify the narrowest supported viewport, text zoom, long labels, localization pressure, empty data, and dense data.

## Color, imagery, and material

Give every color a role: canvas, surface, text, muted text, accent, focus, success, warning, and danger where relevant. Preserve contrast in default, hover, active, disabled, and overlay states.

Prefer product media, domain artifacts, custom illustration, generated assets with a defined art direction, or documented archival sources. Prototype placeholders should communicate the intended subject and treatment. Filters and ambient effects support image coherence; they do not substitute for relevant imagery.

## Interaction and motion

Animation should explain causality, preserve spatial context, focus attention, or establish atmosphere at one orchestrated moment. Choose an intensity consistent with the contract:

- **Restrained:** state transitions and direct feedback.
- **Expressive:** one signature sequence plus purposeful micro-interactions.
- **Cinematic:** choreographed narrative motion with performance and reduced-motion alternatives.

Clickable cards and media need perceivable hover, focus, and active feedback. Effects must preserve access to content when animation is unavailable.

## Language

Write from the user's side of the screen. Use concrete nouns, active verbs, sentence case, and consistent action names. Labels identify; descriptions explain; examples demonstrate. Empty and error states state what happened and the next available action. Claims must be supported by product evidence.

## Final critique

Ask:

1. Which decision could have been produced for any product in this category?
2. Does the signature express the thesis or merely decorate it?
3. Is any effect competing with comprehension or with the signature?
4. Does the mobile composition preserve the intended hierarchy?

Revise the most generic answer and remove the weakest accessory.
